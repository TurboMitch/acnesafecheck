How the acne safe ingredient checker works

This is one combined comedogenic ingredient checker and pore-clogging ingredient checker: it scans your ingredient list against a database of 550+ ingredients known to be comedogenic or pore-clogging, each scored 0–5 on the standard comedogenicity scale. Higher means a greater chance of clogging pores and triggering breakouts. Ingredients that commonly feed fungal acne (Malassezia) get a 🍄 flag too. Ingredients not in the database are shown separately as "not rated" — they are not necessarily safe or unsafe, just not on common pore-clogging lists.

What is the comedogenic scale?

The comedogenic scale rates how likely an ingredient is to clog pores, from 0 (non-comedogenic) to 5 (highly comedogenic). It originated from dermatology research that applied ingredients to skin and observed comedone (clogged-pore) formation (Fulton et al., 1984). A higher number means a greater chance of blackheads, whiteheads and breakouts for acne-prone skin — read more on what comedogenic means.

How to read your results

Ingredients near the top of an INCI list are present in the highest concentration, so a comedogenic ingredient listed first matters more than one near the end. And a single ingredient's score doesn't guarantee how a finished formula behaves (Draelos & DiNardo, 2006) — use ratings as a risk signal, and patch test anything new.

Frequently asked questions

What does "comedogenic" mean?

Comedogenic ingredients tend to block pores, which can lead to comedones (blackheads and whiteheads) and breakouts. Ratings run from 0 (won't clog pores) to 5 (very likely to clog pores).

Is a high-rated ingredient always bad for me?

No. Many people use coconut oil or cocoa butter with no problems. Ratings are a starting point — concentration and your own skin matter a lot.

Where do the ratings come from?

From published comedogenicity research and dermatology references — primarily Fulton et al. (1984) and the re-evaluation by Draelos & DiNardo (2006), plus widely-used esthetician reference lists. We're transparent that these are estimates, not lab results for every formula — full methodology on the about page.

Is this the same as a comedogenic or pore-clogging checker?

Yes — "acne safe checker", "comedogenic ingredient checker" and "pore-clogging ingredient checker" all describe this one tool. It rates every recognised ingredient 0–5 on the comedogenicity scale and flags fungal-acne triggers in the same scan.
