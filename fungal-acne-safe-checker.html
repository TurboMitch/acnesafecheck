What feeds fungal acne?

Malassezia is a yeast that lives on everyone's skin. In fungal acne it overgrows and feeds primarily on fatty acids with a chain length of roughly C11–C24. In practice that means most plant oils, butters, fatty acids and fatty alcohols, many esters (such as those ending in -stearate, -oleate, -palmitate, -myristate), polysorbates, sorbitan esters, lecithin, and fermented ingredients can all act as fuel.

Common fungal-acne trigger ingredients to look for

  • Oils & butters: coconut oil, shea butter, olive oil, jojoba oil, argan oil — almost every plant oil feeds the yeast, even the non-comedogenic ones.
  • Fatty acids & alcohols: stearic acid, lauric acid, cetyl alcohol, cetearyl alcohol.
  • Esters: isopropyl myristate, glyceryl stearate, ethylhexyl palmitate — anything ending in -stearate, -oleate, -palmitate, -myristate.
  • Emulsifiers: polysorbate 80, polysorbate 20, sorbitan oleate, lecithin.
  • Ferments: galactomyces ferment filtrate, bifida ferment lysate, saccharomyces ferment.

Not sure it's fungal acne? Quick self-check

Fungal acne tends to be small, uniform, itchy bumps on the forehead, hairline, chest or back that flare after sweating and ignore normal acne treatment. If that sounds familiar, run your routine through this checker; if your breakouts are mixed whiteheads, blackheads and cysts on cheeks and jawline, it's more likely regular acne — use the comedogenic checker. How to read your results

Every flagged ingredient can feed Malassezia, but position matters: a trigger in the first five ingredients is a far bigger problem than one at the very end. Many people tolerate one minor trigger low on the list; a leave-on product with several triggers near the top is the one to swap out first. Rinse-off products (cleansers, shampoos) are more forgiving than creams and serums that stay on the skin.

What is safe?

Pure hydrocarbons such as squalane, mineral oil and petrolatum don't feed Malassezia, and short-chain esters like caprylic/capric triglyceride are generally considered safe. Most water-based humectants, actives (niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, BHA), silicones and clays are fine.

Frequently asked questions

What ingredients should I avoid for fungal acne?

Anything that feeds Malassezia: most plant oils and butters, C11–C24 fatty acids and fatty alcohols, esters ending in -stearate/-oleate/-palmitate/-myristate, polysorbates, sorbitan esters, lecithin and fermented ingredients.

Which ingredients are fungal acne safe?

Squalane, mineral oil, petrolatum, caprylic/capric triglyceride, most silicones, clays, glycerin, hyaluronic acid, niacinamide and salicylic acid are generally considered Malassezia-safe.

Is "fungal acne safe" the same as "non-comedogenic"?

No. Non-comedogenic = unlikely to clog pores; fungal-acne safe = doesn't feed the yeast. Squalane is both, coconut oil is neither, and several low-comedogenic esters still feed Malassezia. Check both if you're not sure which breakout type you have.

Can a product with one trigger still work for me?

Often yes, especially if the trigger is far down the list or the product is rinse-off. Start by removing the leave-on products with triggers high on the ingredient list, then reintroduce one product at a time.
